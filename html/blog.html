As Write as Snow

Hello! My name is Edythe Millhouse, an aspiring author. I decided to start this blog so that I could get my name out there and give my loyal followers updates throughout my writing process. Here you will find little things about me, my work, and what's to come in the future. I hope you stick around!

Edythe Millhouse - September 23rd, 20XX

The Empty House

Opal Faye Millhouse. A true angel among mortals has now left this wretched land of the living to go dance among the stars. Although I doubt great grandma Opal is doing much dancing. She was a gem much like her name suggests. She taught me everything I know. She comforted me when my family didn’t even notice my tears and she brought me the most precious gift I could’ve ever asked for, my love of Sherlock Holmes. Great grandma Opal will be missed by many, but she won’t be as cherished or beloved to anyone else but me. We understood each other, Opal and I. Two peas in the same pod. She told me stories about how her older sister, Dawn, was everyone’s favorite and could do no wrong, much like the youngest in our family. Opal had to carve her own path in life, and she taught me to do the same. Such a woman could never be forgotten, even long after she’s gone. Mr. Millhouse went to clean out her house the other day. He said he was going to get most of it, but I was able to collect a few of her most precious things that she would’ve wanted me to have, like her many journals describing her exciting life adventures and her own murder mystery ideas, and her original collection of Sherlock Holmes books. They were the only things she held closest to her chest, and nobody else with the Millhouse name would ever want something so trivial as a book. Grandma Opal always said that books held more value than any other treasures on the earth, and I will live by that statement until my own dying breath, when I can finally see my beloved Grandma Opal again and tell her of all the amazing things I was able to accomplish in life. I’m not much into religion, but every once in a while since her passing, I’ve felt her close. The warm touch of her hand on my shoulder, in reassurance as I snuggled with Moriarty and cried about missing her. Or the sudden smell of cookies that she’d make every year around my birthday. She was one of the only people who ever remembered, despite Edward and I sharing it.

Edythe Millhouse - November 14th, 20XX

The Next Chapter on Baker Street

It has been hard to write lately. Ever since my Grandma Opal’s passing, it’s felt impossible to pick up a pen, eat, or even spend time with the few people I care about. A few weeks ago, Travis invited the family to Jonathan Jr.’s first big birthday bash. I would’ve rather gouged my own eyes out with a rusty spoon, so instead I went to Nicole’s more humble and small birthday party for her little girl. Edward, Nora, and Aileen were there too, which was nice. I felt distant from everything the entire time, more than usual. Nothing felt right. Edward, that annoying snob, asked how I was doing. While it was nice to be seen, I wanted to be left alone. Edward knows me better than that, unfortunately. There are many downsides to being a twin, him knowing my thoughts and feelings better than I do is a big one. We talked about Grandma Opal. He knows how close I was to my sweet muse. I also did something that I haven’t done in years. I cried. Edward was kind, probably out of pity for his miserable sister, born 2 minutes after him. My comfort didn’t come from anything that Oaf said, especially since all he did was give me the number for a local therapist’s office, but from what Aileen told me later. She told me about how Edward got her my last book for Christmas last year and how she read it within a week and loved every bit of it. Then she asked me about what ideas I had for my next book! I honestly hadn’t been thinking about writing. I haven’t even considered making a third book until little Aileen said she wanted it. It made me feel seen, my work loved, and my heart ached . I thought I’d quit after Opal’s death. Now I had to keep going. I realized that for Aileen, I was much like my great grandma Opal was to me, an inspiration and an idol. If I couldn’t keep writing for myself, then I’d have to keep writing for Aileen, for Opal, and for anybody else that reads my work and decides to keep creating because of it. So for you, my dear Aileen, I will write.
